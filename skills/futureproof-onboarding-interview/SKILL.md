# FutureProof Onboarding Interview

## When to use

Use this skill when a new FutureProof client is getting set up, or whenever the user asks to "run my onboarding interview," "build my business memory," or "set up FutureProof." The output of this session becomes the client's FutureProof memory — the connective tissue every other FutureProof skill reads.

## Quick reference

- Four stages: 1 Frame -> 2 Interview loop -> 3 Coverage map -> 4 Synthesis & save. It's a conversation, not a form.
- Subject = THE BUSINESS, not the founder's life story. The founder only matters where their thinking is load-bearing for how the business runs.
- Length: budget ~2 hours. One question at a time; push back on anything generic.
- Output: structured memory content in the user's own words, saved to FutureProof via the connector (chat artifact only as fallback).
- Output skeleton: read `references/memory-template.md` in this skill's directory.

## Procedure

Run as four stages: 1 Frame -> 2 Interview loop -> 3 Coverage map -> 4 Synthesis & save. It's a conversation, not a form — Stage 3 is the set of areas you must eventually reach, but you get there by following the thread, not marching down the list.

### Stage 1 — Frame (first ~2 minutes)

1.1 Welcome them and set expectations out loud: roughly two hours, one question at a time, and you'll push back when answers go shallow or generic.
1.2 Say why it's deliberate: "The website version of your business is useless here — I want the real version." Get a verbal "ready" before starting.

### Stage 2 — The interview loop (repeat for every question)

2.1 Ask exactly ONE question. Never stack two questions into one turn.
2.2 Listen, then choose the next question from what they actually said — not from a script.
2.3 Score the answer before moving on: is it specific and true only of THEM, or could it describe fifty other businesses?
2.4 If it's shallow / jargon / a segment instead of a person / contradicts something earlier -> push back ONCE, warmly but directly, then re-ask. Be specific: not "tell me more," but "that works for any competitor — what's the version only true for you?"
2.5 Log any signal that surfaces (a real customer phrase, a contradiction, an "it lives in my head" admission) and bring it back when its area comes up.

### Stage 3 — Coverage map (reach all ten; each has a bar to clear)

For each area, the line below is your OPENING move — keep digging until the answer clears the bar.
   - What it does — on a Tuesday, plain English, no category labels. BAR: a stranger could picture the actual work.
   - Why it exists — the problem solved + why existing options weren't good enough. BAR: a specific inadequacy, not "they were bad."
   - Who it serves — one real human, not a segment: state on arrival, their words, fears, wins, what they tried before. BAR: you could spot them in a room.
   - What ships — the real deliverable + process; what a client gets for their money. BAR: a concrete artifact + steps.
   - Where it's going — success at 12 months and 3 years; what would let the founder walk away happy. BAR: a number or a named outcome.
   - What it stands for — the non-negotiables; what it won't do even for money. BAR: a real line they've actually held.
   - What makes it different — the version they'd tell a competitor over a beer. BAR: not repeatable by a rival.
   - How it sounds — voice, tone, banned words, signature phrases. BAR: you could ghostwrite a sentence they'd approve.
   - How it operates — team, roles, processes that work vs. processes that live in someone's head. BAR: named owners + where it breaks.
   - Where the gaps are — what slows it down, what's undocumented, the founder-shaped ceiling. BAR: an honest weak point, not a humble-brag.

### Stage 4 — Synthesis & save (the output)

4.1 When every area has cleared its bar, stop interviewing.
4.2 Produce structured memory content in the CLIENT'S OWN WORDS, organized by section (what the business does, customer, voice, principles, team & ops, gaps...). Skeleton: `references/memory-template.md` in this skill's directory.
4.3 Show it to the client and get an explicit "yes, that's us" (or corrections) before saving anything.
4.4 Save it through the FutureProof connector — use the connector's save/update tools (e.g. save_context / update_instructions) so the memory lands in their FutureProof space directly, section by section. Only if the FutureProof connector is not available in this conversation, fall back to presenting the full memory content as a chat artifact with instructions to paste it into FutureProof.
4.5 Append a short, honest "still thin" note — what's missing, framed as a heads-up, not homework.

## Pitfalls

- Drifting into the founder's biography when it doesn't explain the business. Redirect to the company.
- Accepting marketing-speak. If it sounds like the About page, dig for the real version.
- Running it like a form. The value is in following the thread, not covering fields in order.
- Saving without approval. The client signs off on the synthesis (4.3) before anything is written to FutureProof.

## Verification

The session is done when the section-organized memory content — in the client's own words — is saved to FutureProof via the connector (or delivered as the fallback artifact), AND the client has a short, honest note on what's still thin or missing (as a heads-up, not homework).
